Inverse planning: shape the dose

A simplified one-dimensional cross-section contains a PTV, a neighbouring OAR and other tissue. Three independently adjustable beam components deposit dose in different spatial patterns. The optimiser changes their non-negative intensities to balance target coverage, hotspots and OAR sparing.

Under the hood: dose matrix, objective and algorithm

Each row of A describes an equal-volume voxel; each column represents a synthetic beam component. The coefficients describe dose per unit intensity. The model has 80 voxels and three components. The PTV uses a minimum-dose penalty at 50 Gy (weight 1) and maximum-dose penalty at 53 Gy (weight 0.6). The OAR uses a one-sided maximum-dose penalty at the selected dose and weight. Other tissue uses a dose-squared penalty at weight 0.025. Each term is averaged across its own structure; weighting and normalisation vary across real systems. The gradient is calculated analytically and projected gradient descent with backtracking accepts non-increasing cost steps. Real planning includes 3D anatomy, machine constraints, delivery complexity, dose calculation accuracy, uncertainty and independent clinical review. The toy model has none of these.

D95 uses the dose of the lowest-ranked voxel still covering at least 95% of the PTV by volume (discrete nearest-rank convention). V50 is the percentage receiving ≥50 Gy. OAR maximum is a single-voxel maximum, not a near-maximum volume metric.
