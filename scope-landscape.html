Local minima, Newton and simulated annealing

A smooth cost landscape can have several valleys. Compare three search methods on the same one-dimensional beam-weight problem. A low cost is not necessarily the lowest possible cost (and even a global minimum is only optimal for the chosen model).

Gradient: x ← x − αF′(x)
Newton: x ← x − F′(x)/F″(x)
Annealing: P(accept uphill) = exp(−ΔF/T)

All searches are bounded to 70–145% for this exercise. Newton uses the curvature when it is nonzero; it can move uphill or converge to a maximum. Annealing uses a reproducible pseudo-random sequence; success is not guaranteed.

Try it: Start at 88% on the two-valley curve. Compare gradient descent with annealing; reduce the initial temperature and repeat. On the bowl, Newton reaches the minimum in one step, whereas gradient descent typically takes several. Try Newton near the ridge and observe why zero slope alone is insufficient.
