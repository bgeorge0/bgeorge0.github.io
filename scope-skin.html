The surface build-up trap

In photon planning, a superficial region may receive less dose per unit fluence because of build-up. A naive uniform-dose objective can compensate by increasing local fluence. This independent two-voxel example deliberately leaves out scatter, geometry, movement and real depth-dose physics.

Dsurface = k × f
F(f) = w × (kf − 50)² + λf²
f* = 50wk/(wk² + λ)

Deep reference fluence and dose are fixed at 50 illustrative units and 50 Gy.

The final metric is an alternative efficiency stress test, not a predicted dose. The model does not recommend editing PTVs, using virtual bolus or changing flash in clinical practice.

Try it: With k = 0.4 and no fluence penalty, optimise and compare the superficial fluence with the deep reference. Increase λ and reoptimise. Discuss how an objective that looks reasonable mathematically can produce an undesirable fluence pattern.
